Cataracts: Understanding Your Vision Needs

A cataract is a condition in which the clear lens of the eye becomes cloudy, leading to blurred or reduced vision. It commonly occurs with aging when proteins in the lens break down and clump together, reducing the lens’s transparency. The lens, located behind the iris, normally helps focus light onto the retina for clear vision. Symptoms include blurry vision, difficulty seeing at night, sensitivity to bright lights, faded colors, and frequent changes in eyeglass power. Although it develops gradually and is painless, untreated cataracts can significantly affect daily activities. However, vision can usually be restored through a safe and effective surgical procedure that replaces the cloudy lens with an artificial one.

Common Symptoms of Cataracts

Cataracts develop gradually and cause cloudy vision as the eye’s natural lens becomes opaque. As the condition progresses, everyday activities such as reading or driving may become difficult. Early detection is important for timely medical consultation and preventing further vision loss.

  • Increased Glare from Lights:
  • Difficulty Seeing at Night:
